# Rice (Paddy) — Pest & Disease Advisory

## Brown Plant Hopper (BPH)
BPH sucks sap from the base of tillers and causes hopperburn — drying of tillers starting
from the tip. Risk is highest in dense, over-fertilized canopies during humid weather.
Management: conserve spiders and mirid bugs (natural enemies), keep bunds and field margins
weeded, avoid late excess nitrogen, and drain the field briefly when populations approach
threshold levels. Yellow sticky traps help monitor arrivals.

## Blast Disease
Caused by a fungus; appears as spindle-shaped (diamond) leaf lesions with grey centres and
brown borders, and neck rot at the panicle stage. Favored by cool nights, high humidity and
excess nitrogen. Management: use resistant varieties where available, avoid dense spacing,
drain standing water, and apply recommended fungicides only at symptom onset.

## Sheath Blight
Water-soaked greenish-grey lesions on leaf sheaths near the waterline, common in
high-density, high-nitrogen crops. Management: wider spacing, balanced nitrogen, border
drainage, and removal of infected stubble after harvest.

## Stem Borer
Whiteheads (dead central shoots) at tillering, and later empty "dead hearts" and
"white ears". Management: install light traps, keep water depth 5-8 cm during peak
egg-laying, avoid ratooning, and destroy stubble after harvest.

## General Crop Calendar (kharif)
Sow in June-July with onset of monsoon; transplant 20-25 day seedlings at 2-3 seedlings
per hill; harvest 120-150 days after transplanting when 80-85% grains turn golden.
